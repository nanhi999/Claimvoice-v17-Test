Partial Theft in Car Insurance: Is It Covered?

Parts stolen from a vehicle • Technical terms explained simply

Partial theft means a part or component is stolen from the insured vehicle while the vehicle itself remains at the location. It is different from total theft, where the entire vehicle is stolen.

Examples of partial theft

  • Alloy wheels or tyres stolen
  • Battery stolen
  • ECU or electronic module stolen
  • Headlamps or mirrors stolen
  • Other covered vehicle components stolen

Own Damage cover

Own Damage (OD)
In simple words: cover for loss or damage to your own insured vehicle, subject to the policy.

Whether a particular stolen part is payable depends on the policy, the part, exclusions, deductibles, depreciation treatment and the facts of the incident.

What should you do after partial theft?

  1. Take photographs before anything is repaired or replaced, where safe.
  2. Inform the insurer promptly.
  3. Report the theft to the police where required.
  4. Keep the FIR/police complaint and photographs.
  5. Do not throw away damaged or remaining evidence without checking whether the insurer needs it.
  6. Ask the insurer/garage what inspection is required.

Example: four wheels stolen

Situation: You find your car parked outside your home with all four wheels missing. The vehicle itself is still there. This is a partial-theft situation. The claim may involve the stolen parts, damage caused during removal and the applicable policy terms.

Why can a partial-theft claim be disputed?

Possible questions can include whether the loss is covered, whether the part was an original/declared item, the condition and age of the part, evidence of theft, applicable deductible and depreciation, and whether any policy exclusion applies.

Important: Do not treat a police FIR as a guarantee that the insurer must pay. The police report establishes the reported incident; the insurer still assesses coverage under the policy.

Documents commonly useful

  • Policy copy
  • Registration certificate
  • FIR/police complaint, where applicable
  • Photographs/video of the vehicle
  • Repair estimate
  • Invoices for replaced parts
  • Any proof showing the vehicle and affected parts before the incident, where available

If the insurer rejects the claim

Ask for the rejection reason and the exact policy clause. If the insurer says the stolen item is not covered, ask them to identify the relevant wording. If the issue is depreciation or deductible, ask for the item-wise calculation.
