Why Motor Insurance Claims Get Rejected — ClaimVoice Topic Hub

This guide is about possible reasons for an objection, rejection, repudiation, reduction or dispute. A circumstance listed here does not automatically mean that the insurer is entitled to reject every claim. The policy wording, facts, evidence, applicable rules and connection between the alleged breach and the loss can matter.

Important ClaimVoice principle: “The insurer has raised an objection” and “the claim is legally/contractually not payable” are not necessarily the same thing. Always ask for the exact reason, the relevant policy wording or condition, and the evidence relied upon.

1. Vehicle modifications & equipment

SituationWhy it can create a problemWhat may be examinedWhat the policyholder should check
Aftermarket accessoriesThe accessory may not automatically have the same coverage as the insured vehicle.Whether it was declared, insured and covered; whether it was involved in the loss.Policy schedule, endorsements, invoices and accessory coverage.
Vehicle modificationsA modification may alter the declared risk, policy conditions or legal status of the vehicle.Nature of modification, disclosure, approval and relevance to the loss.Whether it was disclosed/endorsed and whether it contributed to the incident.
Electrical fittings / non-standard wiringUnapproved or poorly installed electrical work can become particularly relevant in fire or electrical-loss disputes.Wiring, installation, workmanship, cause of loss and evidence.Invoices, installation records and any evidence about the cause of damage.
Engine / ECU / performance changesPerformance changes may affect the risk or policy disclosures.Nature of modification and policy terms.Disclosure, endorsement and whether the modification is connected to the loss.
Tyre, wheel, suspension or structural changesSome changes may raise questions about safety, legality or declared vehicle specifications.Type of change, approval, legality and causation.Invoices, approvals, specifications and policy wording.
CNG/LPG or fuel-system conversionConversion can raise questions about disclosure, approval and installation.Registration records, installation and cause of loss.RC endorsement, installation documentation and policy disclosures.

2. How the vehicle was being used

SituationWhy it can create a problemWhat may be examinedWhat the policyholder should check
Private car used as taxi / commercial vehicleThe actual use may differ from the use permitted or declared under the policy.What the vehicle was being used for at the time of loss, policy wording and relevant permits.Policy schedule, registration category, permit and circumstances of the incident.
Hire or reward useCarrying passengers or goods for payment may be outside permitted private use.Purpose of journey and evidence of payment or commercial use.Actual use at the time of the loss and applicable policy terms.
Racing / speed testingSpecial uses may be excluded or restricted.What the vehicle was doing immediately before the incident.Relevant exclusions and factual evidence.
OverloadingExcess occupants or load can create a policy-condition or causation dispute.Occupants, load and whether excess load contributed to the loss.Incident evidence and the applicable policy condition.

3. Driver, licence & legal compliance

SituationWhy it can create a problemWhat may be examinedWhat the policyholder should check
Driving licence issueThe insurer may question whether the driver was legally authorised to drive the vehicle.Licence validity, class and circumstances at the time of loss.Original licence details and vehicle class.
Permit issueCommercial vehicles can have permit requirements relevant to their use.Permit validity and actual use.Permit documents and policy conditions.
Drunk / drugged driving allegationApplicable policy restrictions and law may become relevant.Police, medical and other evidence.Exact allegation and evidence relied upon.
Registration / fitness / compliance issueFor applicable vehicles, legal compliance can become part of the dispute.Registration, fitness and other mandatory records.Validity at the relevant time.

4. Claim, evidence & investigation issues

SituationWhy it can create a problemWhat may be examinedWhat the policyholder should check
Late intimationDelay may be cited where it affects investigation or evidence.When the incident occurred, when it was reported and reason for delay.Call records, emails, claim acknowledgement and explanation for delay.
Previous damageThe insurer may argue that some damage existed before the reported incident.Old photographs, inspection records, survey findings and repair history.Evidence separating old and new damage.
Unauthorised repairs before surveyOriginal damage may become harder to assess.Photos, repair invoices and inspection opportunity.Whether the insurer was informed and what evidence remains.
Inconsistent informationDifferences between statements, documents or evidence can trigger investigation.Claim form, police report, statements, photographs and timelines.Keep the account factual and consistent; correct genuine errors promptly.
Non-disclosure / misrepresentationMaterial information may affect underwriting or coverage.Proposal information, policy records and the allegedly undisclosed fact.What was actually asked, answered and recorded.
Suspicious circumstancesInsurers may investigate unusual or inconsistent circumstances.Police records, location evidence, statements, keys, CCTV and other evidence.Request the specific concern rather than accepting a vague allegation.
Missing documentsDocuments can be necessary to assess ownership, loss or circumstances.Which document is missing and why it is required.Ask for a written list and provide relevant documents with acknowledgement.

What should you do if the insurer raises one of these objections?

  1. Ask for the objection in writing.
  2. Ask for the exact policy clause/condition/exclusion being relied upon.
  3. Ask what evidence or finding supports the objection.
  4. Check whether the alleged issue actually existed at the time of the loss.
  5. Check whether the issue is connected to the loss or damage being claimed.
  6. Collect photographs, invoices, RC/permit/licence records, police documents, correspondence and other relevant evidence.
  7. Respond in writing rather than relying only on telephone conversations.
  8. If the matter remains unresolved, use the insurer's grievance process and then consider the appropriate further remedy.

This guide is for consumer awareness and does not determine whether a particular claim is payable. Claim outcomes depend on the policy, facts, evidence and applicable requirements. For a specific dispute, obtain appropriate professional advice where necessary.
