Documents Difficult to Procure for a Motor Insurance Claim: What Can You Do?

ClaimVoice guide • Simple English • Technical terms explained with examples

A claim can become stressful when the insurer asks for a document that is difficult to obtain, takes time to issue, or does not exist in the form being requested. The answer is not always to simply keep sending the same document again.

Key idea: Ask the insurer to clearly state why the document is required, what fact it is meant to prove, and whether another record can establish the same fact.

What is evidence?

Evidence
In simple words: information or records used to support what you say happened.

Evidence can include an FIR, photographs, repair estimates, payment records, GPS records, messages, vehicle documents, survey reports or other records relevant to the loss.

Why can a document become difficult to procure?

  • The issuing authority takes time.
  • The document is held by another person or organisation.
  • The original is lost and a duplicate is required.
  • The event happened in a place different from where the owner lives.
  • The requested record is not applicable to the particular claim.

Example: a document is genuinely unavailable

Situation: An insurer asks for a record from a government office. The policyholder has already applied for it but the office has not issued it. Instead of simply ignoring the request, the policyholder can submit proof of the application, explain the delay in writing and ask whether the insurer will accept an alternative record while the original is pending.

Ask what the document is meant to prove

This is one of the most useful questions a policyholder can ask. A document request should be understood in context. If the insurer wants to verify ownership, for example, sale documents, registration records and payment evidence may all be relevant. If the insurer wants to verify the cause of loss, photographs, survey findings and other records may matter more.

What if you cannot provide the document?

  1. Respond in writing.
  2. Explain why it cannot currently be provided.
  3. Attach proof of your efforts to obtain it.
  4. Ask whether an alternative document is acceptable.
  5. Keep copies of every response.
  6. Ask for a clear written explanation if the claim is later delayed or rejected because of the missing document.

Do not create or alter documents

Important: Never create a false document, change a date, edit a photograph to misrepresent an event, or submit information that you know is incorrect. That can turn a document problem into a much more serious claim issue.

Document request vs claim rejection

A request for additional documents is not the same thing as a final claim rejection. If the insurer ultimately rejects the claim, ask for the reason and the exact policy clause or condition relied upon.

Build a claim evidence file

Keep one folder containing the policy, claim number, emails, letters, photographs, FIR, repair estimates, bills, surveyor communications and every document submitted. A clear timeline can make a complicated claim much easier to understand.
