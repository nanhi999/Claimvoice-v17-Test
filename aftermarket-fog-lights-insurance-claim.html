Aftermarket Fog Lights & Car Insurance Claim Rejection

Important: This is an illustrative claim situation, not a statement about a particular real person's case. An aftermarket accessory does not automatically mean that every claim is rejected. The actual policy wording, endorsements, facts of the loss, evidence and applicable requirements have to be considered.

The situation

You fitted aftermarket fog lights to your car.

The installation involved additional electrical wiring. Later, the car suffered a fire and extensive damage. You reported the loss and submitted an own-damage claim.

The insurer then raised an objection: the vehicle had an aftermarket electrical fitment which had not been declared or endorsed, and the insurer relied on that point while rejecting or disputing the claim.

For a vehicle owner, the natural reaction is: “The fire was genuine. Why does the insurer care about my fog lights?”

Because once an electrical modification is involved in a fire claim, the insurer may examine not only whether the loss happened, but also what caused it, what was modified, whether the change was disclosed or endorsed, and which policy term is being relied upon.

Why can aftermarket fog lights become important?

A fog light may look like a small accessory. But an installation can involve wiring, switches, relays, fuses or connections to the vehicle's existing electrical system.

That becomes particularly relevant when the reported loss itself involves electrical failure or fire.

The insurer may therefore ask:

  • When were the fog lights installed?
  • Who installed them and where?
  • Was the original wiring altered?
  • Was the modification disclosed to the insurer?
  • Was an endorsement required or issued?
  • Where did the fire originate?
  • Is there evidence that the modification caused or contributed to the loss?

Does an aftermarket fitment automatically cancel the claim?

Do not assume that it does.

Equally, do not assume that the insurer is wrong simply because the accident or fire was genuine.

The correct question is:

What exact policy clause, condition, exclusion or other basis is the insurer relying upon, and how does it apply to this particular loss?

There is also an important factual question: was the modification connected to the loss? A modification may be highly relevant to an electrical fire investigation, while a completely unrelated modification may raise a different question in a collision claim.

What does IRDAI say about changes to a vehicle?

IRDAI's motor-insurance guidance says that changes in a policy, including modifications to the vehicle or its use, are recorded by endorsement. It also specifically says that if a CNG/LPG kit is fitted, the insurer should be informed so that the kit can be covered under the relevant sections on payment of additional premium where applicable. Read IRDAI's motor-insurance guidance.

This does not mean that every small accessory has the same treatment. It does mean that a policyholder should check before making a material modification and keep the insurer's written response.

If the insurer rejects the claim, ask these questions

“Please provide the exact policy clause, condition or exclusion relied upon.”
“What specific modification are you relying upon?”
“Are you saying the accessory itself is not covered, or that the entire vehicle claim is not payable?”
“What evidence shows that the modification caused or contributed to the fire?”
“Was an endorsement required for this particular modification? If yes, please identify the relevant requirement.”
“What technical finding establishes the origin and cause of the fire?”
“Please provide the rejection/repudiation decision and reasons in writing.”

Why the cause of the fire matters

“Aftermarket fitment was present” and “aftermarket fitment caused the fire” are not the same factual statement.

If the insurer's position is that the modification caused or contributed to the fire, the policyholder can ask what evidence supports that conclusion. Depending on the case, relevant material may include the surveyor's findings, investigator's report, photographs, wiring condition, fire-origin analysis and other evidence.

Do not destroy or alter the damaged vehicle unnecessarily before the insurer has had a reasonable opportunity to inspect it, unless immediate safety or legal requirements make that unavoidable.

What should you do before fitting electrical accessories?

  1. Ask the insurer whether the proposed modification needs to be declared.
  2. Ask whether an endorsement or additional premium is required.
  3. Use a competent installer.
  4. Keep the purchase and installation invoices.
  5. Keep product specifications and photographs where useful.
  6. Keep the insurer's written response with the policy documents.

IRDAI advises policyholders to have relevant changes recorded by endorsement. Check the current IRDAI guidance.

What about other aftermarket modifications?

The same awareness principle can apply to other modifications, although each situation is different. Examples include:

  • Aftermarket headlights and LED lighting
  • Additional electrical accessories and wiring
  • Sound systems and amplifiers
  • Dash cameras with permanent wiring
  • Parking sensors or reversing cameras
  • CNG/LPG installations
  • Performance or ECU modifications
  • Suspension, wheel or structural changes

Do not assume that all modifications are treated identically. The policy wording, endorsement, nature of the modification and circumstances of the loss matter.

Keep these documents

  • Accessory purchase invoice
  • Installation invoice
  • Product specification, where available
  • Photographs of the installation
  • Any endorsement issued by the insurer
  • Insurance policy and schedule
  • Surveyor/investigator correspondence
  • Written rejection or repudiation letter
  • Fire/police documents where applicable

If the claim remains disputed

First obtain the insurer's decision and reasons in writing. Respond point-by-point with the relevant documents and ask the insurer's grievance mechanism to review the matter if you believe the decision is incorrect or inadequately explained.

Frequently asked questions

Can aftermarket fog lights affect a car insurance claim?

They can become relevant, particularly in an electrical or fire claim, because the insurer may examine the modification, disclosure/endorsement and whether it caused or contributed to the loss. It does not by itself establish the outcome of every claim.

Do aftermarket accessories automatically void car insurance?

No blanket conclusion should be made. The applicable policy wording, endorsement, nature of the accessory, facts of the loss and insurer's stated basis all matter.

What should I ask if my claim is rejected due to an aftermarket modification?

Ask for the exact policy clause or condition, the evidence relied upon, the specific modification concerned, and an explanation of how it affects the particular loss.

Should I inform my insurer before modifying my car?

For material modifications, it is prudent to check with the insurer first and obtain written confirmation about disclosure, endorsement and any additional premium that may apply. IRDAI's motor-insurance guidance addresses recording vehicle modifications by endorsement.

What if the modification had nothing to do with the accident?

Ask the insurer to explain the connection between the modification and the claimed loss and to identify the exact policy provision relied upon. The facts and policy wording determine the outcome.

Quick ClaimVoice checklist

  1. Get the rejection in writing.
  2. Ask for the exact policy clause.
  3. Identify the exact modification.
  4. Ask what evidence connects it to the loss.
  5. Check whether an endorsement was required.
  6. Keep invoices and installation records.
  7. Respond in writing.
  8. Escalate if unresolved.
