Wrong Vehicle Variant in Motor Insurance Policy: If the RC Was Submitted and the Insurer Was Informed, Who Is Responsible?

A vehicle's insurance policy contains important information about the insured vehicle. A mistake in the vehicle variant may appear to be a small administrative error, but it can become a serious issue when the vehicle is involved in an accident.

The situation becomes even more complicated when the Registration Certificate (RC) was submitted for issuance of the policy, the policy contained a wrong variant, and the policyholder subsequently informed the insurer about the mistake and requested correction.

Who is responsible?

There is no responsible answer without examining the complete chain of events. But there are some very important questions that should be asked.

The RC was submitted — so how did the wrong variant enter the policy?

If the RC was submitted as part of the insurance process, a reasonable question arises:

  • Who entered the vehicle information into the insurance proposal or policy?
  • Who checked the information?
  • Who verified the vehicle details?
  • Was the variant recorded correctly in the insurer's system?
  • If the information was entered incorrectly, at what stage did the error occur?

These questions become even more important when the customer later specifically reports the discrepancy to the insurer.

The customer's responsibility

A policyholder is not free from responsibility. The customer should provide accurate information, submit the required documents, carefully read the policy when received, check any endorsement and immediately report errors.

But the issue becomes more complicated when the customer reports the error to the insurer before an accident occurs.

What changes when the insurer has already been informed?

Consider this sequence:

RC submitted → Policy issued → Wrong variant noticed → Customer informs insurer in writing → Correction requested → Insurer issues endorsement → Wrong variant still appears → Customer informs insurer again → Grievance complaint filed → Accident occurs before correction.

Can the dispute be looked at simply by saying, “The policy contains the wrong variant”? Or should the entire history be examined?

Who verified the documents?

This is perhaps the most important question.

When an insurance policy is issued, information about the vehicle has to come from somewhere. The insurance process may involve the customer, dealer, intermediary and insurer.

  • Who supplied the information?
  • Who entered it?
  • Who checked it?
  • What documents were available?
  • What did the insurer's records show?
  • When was the insurer first informed about the discrepancy?
  • Who processed the endorsement?
  • Why did the error remain after the correction request?

These questions are especially important where the customer says that the RC had already been submitted for policy issuance.

Why should the customer automatically suffer?

This does not mean that every wrong policy entry is automatically the insurer's responsibility. The facts matter.

If the customer deliberately supplied incorrect information, the situation would obviously be different. If a dealer/intermediary supplied incorrect information, the dealer's role may need examination. If the insurer itself recorded incorrect information despite having the correct documents, that is another issue.

And if the insurer was specifically informed about the error before an accident but the correction remained incomplete, the insurer's records and correspondence become particularly important.

Responsibility should therefore be examined from the entire chain of events — not merely from the final policy document.

What if an accident happens while the correction is pending?

The policyholder should not assume that the claim is automatically rejected. The policyholder should also not assume that the claim is automatically payable.

The correct approach is to notify the insurer immediately, lodge the claim according to the policy requirements, and provide the complete documentary history showing that the discrepancy was reported before the accident.

A simple lesson for every car owner

Whenever you receive a motor insurance policy, compare it with the RC and the actual vehicle. Check the registration number, make, model, variant, fuel type, engine number, chassis number, policy period, IDV, NCB, add-ons, deductible and endorsements.

If anything is wrong: report it in writing.

When the corrected policy or endorsement arrives: check it again.

The ClaimVoice Question

If the RC was submitted for policy issuance, the wrong vehicle variant appeared in the policy, the customer informed the insurer about the error before any accident, and the insurer's subsequent endorsement still showed the wrong variant — who should bear the consequences if an accident occurs during the correction process?

That question deserves to be answered on the basis of the documents, records, correspondence, policy terms and applicable rules.

ClaimVoice believes that a policyholder should take responsibility for checking his policy — but responsibility should not automatically end there.
