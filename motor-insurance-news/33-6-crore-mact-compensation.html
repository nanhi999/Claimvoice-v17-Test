₹33.6 Crore Motor Accident Compensation: How a 1994 Claim Reached Such a Large Amount

ClaimVoice explanation: A Mumbai Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) has directed Oriental Insurance Company Ltd. to pay around ₹33.6 crore, including interest, to the family of Kavita Sawlani, who died in a 1994 road accident. The underlying compensation was recomputed at nearly ₹8.8 crore; the much larger headline figure includes interest at 9% from the date the claim was filed until payment.

This is important because the headline ₹33.6 crore should not be confused with the compensation amount alone. It also illustrates how long-running third-party motor accident claims can create very large interest liabilities.

What happened in 1994?

On 3 July 1994, a car travelling on the Thane-Nashik Highway was involved in a head-on collision with a lorry near Bhiwandi. Kavita Sawlani and her sister Kanchan Melwani died. The family filed a motor accident compensation claim on 23 December 1994.

Oriental Insurance had insured both vehicles. The insurer disputed issues including negligence, Kavita's income and the dependency claimed by the family.

How did the compensation change?

The original tribunal award assessed Kavita's annual income at about ₹70 lakh, based on evidence of her professional work in Dubai, and awarded more than ₹5.5 crore. After the matter went through the Bombay High Court, the compensation was recomputed to nearly ₹8.8 crore.

The latest MACT direction then applied 9% annual interest from 23 December 1994, the date the claim was filed, until payment. That long period is the main reason the final amount is around ₹33.6 crore.

₹8.8 crore compensation vs ₹33.6 crore payable amount

The simple difference

₹8.8 crore ≈ compensation assessed.
₹33.6 crore ≈ compensation plus accumulated interest and the resulting payable amount.

This is not the same as saying that the victim's family was originally awarded ₹33.6 crore as compensation. The reports explain that the large figure includes decades of interest.

Why can interest become so large?

Interest is meant to compensate for the fact that an amount found payable remained unpaid while the legal proceedings continued. Over a very long period, even a single-digit annual rate can add a substantial amount.

For a simple illustration only, 9% of ₹8.8 crore is about ₹79.2 lakh for one year. Over many years, the accumulated interest becomes very large. The actual tribunal calculation should always be taken from the order rather than recreated from a headline figure.

Is this the same as a normal car insurance claim?

No. This is a third-party motor accident compensation matter, not an ordinary own-damage claim for repairing or replacing the policyholder's own car.

  • Own-damage claim: you claim under your own motor policy for covered loss to your insured vehicle.
  • Third-party claim: compensation is sought for injury, death or property damage caused to another person in a road accident, subject to the applicable law and insurance framework.
  • MACT claim: a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal deals with eligible motor accident compensation cases.

What should motorists learn from this?

  • Third-party liability can be financially significant and can continue for many years.
  • Preserve the FIR, accident records, insurance details, driving licence and other evidence carefully.
  • Do not assume that a motor claim is only about repairing your own vehicle.
  • Long-running litigation can involve interest in addition to the underlying compensation.
  • The final amount in a particular case depends on the tribunal/court orders and the facts proved in that case.

Why this matters to ClaimVoice readers

For a vehicle owner, the practical lesson is to understand the difference between own-damage protection and third-party liability. A comprehensive or package policy may protect the insured vehicle against covered own damage, while third-party insurance addresses liabilities imposed by law for harm caused to others.
