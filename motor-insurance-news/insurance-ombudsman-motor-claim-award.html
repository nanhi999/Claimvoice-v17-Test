Insurance Ombudsman Motor Claim Award: What a Used-Two-Wheeler Case Teaches

A Kerala High Court judgment in N J Joseph v. National Insurance Company Ltd. examined an Insurance Ombudsman award concerning a motor policy and a personal accident claim after a used two-wheeler was purchased. The Ombudsman had dismissed the complaint, but the High Court later considered the transfer application and the timing of the accident in a different light.

What happened?

The deceased purchased a two-wheeler on 21 September 2023 and submitted an application for transfer of ownership to the Transport Department on the same day. The registration certificate was issued in his name on 29 September 2023.

On 27 September 2023, before the new RC was issued, he suffered fatal injuries in an accident. The vehicle had an insurance policy that included personal accident cover for the owner-driver.

Why did the insurer reject the claim?

The insurer relied mainly on two points: the claim had been intimated after a delay, and the deceased was not the registered owner on the date of the accident because the RC transfer was completed two days later.

The insurer also argued that the conditions for owner-driver personal accident cover had not been met and that the policy had not been transferred into the purchaser's name.

What did the Insurance Ombudsman decide?

The Insurance Ombudsman passed an award dated 8 April 2025 dismissing the complaint. The award treated registration in the purchaser's name as occurring only on 29 September 2023, after the accident.

What did the Kerala High Court examine?

The High Court noted that the transfer application had been submitted on the very day of purchase and was subsequently approved without the registering authority finding defects in the application.

The Court applied the idea of relation back in the circumstances: in simple words, once the transfer application was validly approved, the legal effect of the approval could relate back to the date on which the application was filed. The Court therefore did not accept the technical argument that the later issue of the RC automatically defeated the claim.

Why is this important for used-car and used-bike buyers?

  • Ownership transfer and insurance transfer are separate practical steps and should be completed promptly.
  • Keep proof of the sale, payment, delivery, transfer application and insurance records.
  • Do not assume that the date printed on the new RC is the only fact that can ever matter in a dispute.
  • Personal accident cover for an owner-driver has specific conditions; it should not automatically be equated with an own-damage claim.
  • A claim dispute can move from the insurer's grievance process to the Insurance Ombudsman and, where appropriate, to a court.

How does the Insurance Ombudsman fit into claim escalation?

The Insurance Ombudsman is an alternative dispute-resolution mechanism for eligible insurance complaints. In practical terms, a policyholder normally raises the complaint with the insurer first and then considers Ombudsman escalation when the matter remains unresolved and the complaint meets the applicable requirements.

An Ombudsman proceeding is not the same thing as a customer story published on ClaimVoice. An Ombudsman award is an official decision/recommendation within the applicable dispute-resolution framework; a Real Claim Story is an individual's account of what happened.

What should a policyholder keep before approaching the Ombudsman?

  • Policy schedule and certificate of insurance
  • Claim form and claim-intimation record
  • Insurer's repudiation/rejection letter or final response
  • Grievance complaint and response
  • RC, sale documents and transfer application evidence where ownership changed
  • FIR, accident documents and medical records where relevant
  • All important email, letter and acknowledgement records
