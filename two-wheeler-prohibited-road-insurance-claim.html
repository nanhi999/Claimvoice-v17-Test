Two-Wheeler Accident on a Prohibited Road or Expressway: Can Insurance Reject the Claim?

Imagine a motorcycle enters a road or expressway where a clearly applicable traffic restriction prohibits two-wheelers. An accident occurs and the motorcycle is damaged. The rider has a valid insurance policy, but the insurer raises the prohibited-road issue. What should the policyholder check before accepting or challenging the decision?

ClaimVoice note: A modification, road restriction or documentation issue does not by itself answer every insurance question. The applicable policy wording, endorsements, facts, evidence and law must be considered for the particular claim.

First establish the road restriction

Not every road called a highway or expressway has the same restrictions. The first question is whether a prohibition actually applied to that road, vehicle class, direction and time. Section 115 of the Motor Vehicles Act allows restrictions or prohibitions on specified classes of motor vehicles on specified roads, subject to the applicable notification and traffic requirements.

A traffic violation and an insurance exclusion are not identical questions

If two-wheelers were prohibited, entering the road can be a traffic-law issue. The insurance question is separate: the insurer should identify the policy term, condition or other legal basis it says affects the particular cover and loss.

If the insurer rejects or restricts the claim

  • Ask for the exact policy clause.
  • Ask which notification/order establishes the road restriction.
  • Ask whether the decision concerns own damage, third-party liability, personal accident or another benefit.
  • Ask how the alleged violation affects the particular loss.
  • Request the decision in writing.

Evidence worth preserving

Keep photographs of entry signs, location information, police records, accident photographs, the policy, RC, driving licence and all insurer correspondence. If the insurer relies on a notification or road order, ask for its details so you can understand exactly what applied.

How to avoid the issue

Before entering a controlled-access road, follow the applicable signs and restrictions. If a two-wheeler is prohibited, use an authorised alternative route. Do not rely on the existence of insurance as permission to use a prohibited road.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are all expressway accidents involving motorcycles automatically rejected?
No blanket conclusion should be made without checking the applicable road restriction, policy wording, facts and legal basis for the insurer's decision.
What should I ask if the insurer says the road was prohibited?
Ask for the exact notification/order, the policy clause relied upon and an explanation of how the restriction affects the particular claim.
Does a genuine accident guarantee payment?
No. The accident is only one part of claim assessment; policy terms, use, exclusions, evidence and applicable law can also matter.
