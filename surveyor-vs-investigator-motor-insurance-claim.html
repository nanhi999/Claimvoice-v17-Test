Surveyor vs Investigator in a Motor Insurance Claim: Duties and Differences

ClaimVoice guide • Simple English • Technical terms explained with examples

Policyholders often hear two words during a difficult claim: surveyor and investigator. They are not automatically the same role.

What is a surveyor?

Surveyor
In simple words: a person appointed to assess the loss or damage and prepare an assessment in accordance with the applicable insurance rules and policy.

For a motor claim, a surveyor may inspect the vehicle, record visible damage, review repair estimates, check the circumstances of the loss and assess the amount of loss relevant to the claim.

What is an investigator?

Investigator
In simple words: a person appointed to verify facts or investigate questions about the circumstances of a claim.

An investigation may look at issues such as the accident circumstances, ownership, vehicle use, driver details, previous damage, timing, documents or other facts that need verification.

Simple example

Accident claim: The surveyor may inspect the damaged bumper, bonnet and headlamp and assess the repair or replacement work. If there is a question about whether the damage occurred in the reported accident, an investigator may be asked to verify the circumstances.

Can both be involved in one claim?

Yes. A claim can involve a surveyor and, where the insurer considers further verification necessary, an investigator. Their roles can overlap in the information they review, but their functions are not identical.

What should a policyholder do?

  • Cooperate with reasonable inspection and information requests.
  • Give the same factual account consistently.
  • Keep original documents and copies.
  • Ask for important requests in writing.
  • Do not guess when you do not know an answer.
  • Ask the insurer to explain the specific issue if the claim is questioned.

Does an investigator decide the claim?

An investigation report is not, by itself, the same thing as the final claim decision. The insurer makes the claim decision under the applicable policy and rules, taking relevant reports and evidence into account.

What if you disagree with the findings?

Do not simply write “I disagree.” Identify the exact fact you dispute. Provide documents, photographs, statements or other evidence that address that point. If the insurer rejects the claim, ask for the written reason and the policy provision relied upon.
