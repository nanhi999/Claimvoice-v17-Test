Motor Insurance Claim Documents Checklist: What You May Need

ClaimVoice guide • Simple English • Technical terms explained with examples

The exact documents needed depend on the type of claim, policy and circumstances. There is no single checklist that fits every accident, theft or fire claim. Use this as a practical starting point.

Common documents

  • Motor insurance policy or policy number
  • Registration Certificate (RC)
  • Driving licence of the driver
  • Claim form, where required
  • Photographs and videos of the vehicle and scene
  • Repair estimate and relevant bills
  • FIR or police report where applicable
  • Vehicle keys, especially for theft claims
  • Bank details for settlement, where required
  • Any additional document specifically requested for the claim

Accident claim

Keep photographs of the damage, accident location and other vehicles where safe. The insurer may also need the driver's details, RC, licence and repair estimate.

Theft claim

FIR (First Information Report)
In simple words: a police record of the reported offence or incident, where an FIR is registered.

For a vehicle theft claim, police records, keys, RC, policy details and other documents may be important. The insurer may ask for additional records during verification.

Partial theft

If parts are stolen but the vehicle remains at the location, photograph the vehicle before repairs and report the incident as required. Keep the list of missing parts and repair/replacement estimate.

Fire claim

Keep photographs of the vehicle, fire damage and surrounding area where safe. Depending on the circumstances, police, fire-service, forensic or other records may become relevant.

Total Loss or CTL

Insured Declared Value (IDV)
In simple words: the insured value shown in the policy and used as an important reference for certain total-loss or theft settlements, subject to the policy terms.

For a total-loss or theft claim, the insurer may require ownership records, keys, police documents and other evidence before settlement.

Keep a submission record

Whenever you submit documents, keep a copy and record the date. If documents are sent by email or uploaded online, save the acknowledgement or submission confirmation.

If the insurer asks for more documents

Ask what each additional document is for and whether a substitute record can be accepted if the document is genuinely unavailable. Do not ignore a reasonable request, but do keep a written record of what you supplied.
