Vehicle Recovered After Theft Insurance Claim: What Should You Do?

If your stolen car or two-wheeler has been recovered after you already informed the insurer or lodged a theft claim, do not assume the matter is automatically finished. Recovery can change the next steps, and the police, court, insurer and vehicle owner may each have a role.

Quick action: Inform the insurer and police/recovery authorities promptly, keep every recovery document, and avoid repairing or changing the vehicle before the insurer has had a reasonable opportunity to inspect it where inspection is required.

1. What should you do immediately after recovery?

  1. Confirm the recovery with the police and obtain the relevant recovery/seizure documentation.
  2. Inform the insurer in writing and quote the existing claim number.
  3. Ask the insurer whether an inspection is required before the vehicle is repaired or released for work.
  4. Photograph the vehicle as recovered, including damage, broken locks, ignition, wiring, missing parts and any other visible signs.
  5. Keep the keys, documents and police papers safely.

2. What happens to a theft insurance claim after recovery?

It depends on the stage of the claim and the policy terms. A theft claim can involve verification of the theft, ownership or insurable interest, police records, keys, vehicle condition and recovery status. If the insurer has not yet paid the theft claim, recovery should be reported immediately so the insurer can decide what further assessment is required.

If the insurer has already paid the theft claim, do not assume you can simply take possession and treat the settlement as cancelled. The insurer may have acquired contractual or recovery rights depending on the policy, settlement documents and applicable law. Ask the insurer in writing what documents and actions are required before taking or retaining possession.

3. How do you get the recovered vehicle released from court?

If the vehicle is connected with a criminal case, it may be in police custody or subject to court proceedings. The exact procedure varies with the case and local practice. Usually, the lawful claimant works through the investigating police officer and the concerned court for an order permitting release.

What is Superdari?

Superdari (also called interim custody in simple terms) generally means the court permits a person entitled to possession to keep the vehicle temporarily, subject to conditions. It does not necessarily mean that the criminal case itself has ended.

Do not drive away a court-held vehicle merely because the police say it has been recovered. Follow the court/police release process and keep a copy of the release order and acknowledgement.

4. Documents normally required

The exact list can vary, but commonly relevant records may include:

  • FIR or theft complaint and recovery report
  • Police recovery/seizure memo or panchnama, where issued
  • Court release/Superdari order, where applicable
  • RC and identity/ownership documents
  • Insurance policy and claim number
  • Keys and available spare key
  • Insurer correspondence
  • Vehicle inspection report, estimate or job card, if prepared
  • RTO documents requested for the particular case

For a practical checklist, see the Theft Claim Documents guide and Forms & Downloads.

5. Do not repair the vehicle before inspection when inspection is required

A recovered vehicle can contain important evidence. If the insurer wants to inspect it, give the insurer a reasonable opportunity to do so before dismantling, repainting, changing wiring or carrying out major repairs.

Take clear photographs first. If urgent safety work is unavoidable, document the condition before work and keep estimates, invoices, job cards and replaced parts where practical.

6. What if the recovered vehicle is damaged?

Recovery does not mean the vehicle will necessarily be returned in the same condition in which it was stolen. It may have broken locks, damaged ignition components, missing parts, accident damage or other damage.

Ask the insurer in writing how the damage will be assessed under the existing claim and whether a separate assessment or claim process is required. Do not assume that every post-theft damage item will automatically be payable.

7. The important security step: check the locks and security system

⚠️ Recovery is not the end of the security problem

A thief may have had access to the vehicle, keys or security components. Get the door locks, ignition/lock cylinder, keys, immobiliser, steering lock, alarm and other relevant security components professionally checked.

If a component appears compromised, replacing it is a sensible security precaution. This is not a statement that IRDAI universally mandates lock replacement after every theft recovery.

If you use an aftermarket lock/security component

  • Use a reputable professional.
  • Keep the genuine purchase invoice/bill.
  • Keep the job card or fitting record.
  • Keep photographs and details of the work where practical.

If you use an authorised service centre

  • Keep the invoice and job card.
  • Where available, keep the service record showing the work.
  • The work may also be verifiable through the manufacturer's authorised service history, depending on the vehicle and how the service centre records it.

These records can be useful if questions later arise about what security precautions were taken after recovery.

8. What if the insurer asks for additional documents?

Ask for the request in writing. Submit what is reasonably available and explain in writing if a document is not yet available because the police, court, RTO or another authority has not issued it. Keep proof of submission.

Do not ignore repeated document requests, but you can ask the insurer to explain why a particular document is relevant to the claim.

9. What if the insurer rejects the claim?

Ask for the rejection/repudiation decision in writing and identify the exact policy condition or exclusion relied upon. Then compare the reason with the facts and evidence.

If you believe the decision is wrong, use the Claim Reconsideration Request and follow the Claim Escalation Guide.

10. What if the insurer does not pay or does not respond?

Start with the insurer's grievance process and keep a dated record of every communication. If the matter remains unresolved, you can consider escalation through IRDAI Bima Bharosa. Bima Bharosa is IRDAI's grievance platform and allows policyholders to register and track complaints.

Where the matter falls within the Insurance Ombudsman framework and its eligibility requirements, information about the Ombudsman and filing routes is available from the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen. Always check the current rules and eligibility before filing.

11. What is a Non-Traceable / Final Police Report?

A non-traceable or final report is a police record used in appropriate cases to report the status of investigation. It is not simply the same thing as saying “the vehicle was not found.” The insurer may ask for a court-certified or otherwise accepted copy depending on the claim and its process.

If the vehicle is later recovered, inform the insurer and police/court authorities about the recovery. Do not rely on an old non-traceable status without updating the relevant parties.

12. Practical checklist after recovery

☐ Keep recovery papers
☐ Follow court/Superdari process
☐ Photograph recovered condition
☐ Arrange insurer inspection if required
☐ Check locks and ignition
☐ Check keys/security system
☐ Replace compromised components
☐ Keep bills and job cards
