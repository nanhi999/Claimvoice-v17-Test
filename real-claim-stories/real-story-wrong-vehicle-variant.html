Policy Shows Wrong Vehicle Variant Even After Correction Request: Who Will Be Responsible If an Accident Happens?

Customer Submitted Real Story

A customer purchased a used car from a used-car dealer. The insurance policy for the vehicle was also purchased through the dealer.

At the time of policy issuance, the vehicle's Registration Certificate (RC) was submitted for the insurance policy.

After receiving the policy, the customer noticed that the vehicle variant mentioned in the policy did not match the actual vehicle.

The customer did not ignore the discrepancy. He immediately informed the insurance company by email and requested that the policy be corrected.

After some time, the insurance company issued an endorsement/revised policy.

The customer did not immediately check every detail of the revised policy. Since he had specifically requested the correction and the insurer had issued an endorsement, he assumed that the required correction had been made.

A few days later, after reading information on the ClaimVoice website about the importance of checking motor insurance policies, he decided to check the policy again.

He was shocked to find that the endorsed policy was still showing a different vehicle variant.

The customer immediately contacted the insurer again and requested correction.

When the issue was still not resolved, he escalated the matter to the insurer's grievance department.

The grievance is currently pending and the customer is waiting for the insurer's response.

The question now is very simple

What happens if an accident occurs while the policy still shows the wrong vehicle variant?

Who will be responsible?

The customer accepts that he should have checked the revised policy carefully. But there is another important side to the matter.

The RC had already been submitted for issuance of the policy.

The customer had subsequently specifically informed the insurer that the vehicle variant recorded in the policy was incorrect.

The insurer was therefore put on notice about the discrepancy.

An endorsement was subsequently issued. Yet, according to the customer, the wrong variant continued to appear in the endorsed policy.

Who entered and verified the vehicle details?

  • Who entered the vehicle details?
  • Who checked those details against the documents submitted for the policy?
  • Who verified the vehicle variant?
  • If the RC was available, how did the wrong variant enter the policy?
  • Once the customer informed the insurer about the mistake, who was responsible for correcting it?
  • If the insurer issued an endorsement, why did the incorrect variant remain?

Why should the customer suffer for an error he had already reported?

The customer is not saying that a policyholder has no responsibility. A customer should provide correct information and should carefully check the policy.

But in this case, according to the customer:

  • The RC was submitted.
  • The policy was issued.
  • The discrepancy was noticed.
  • The insurer was informed in writing.
  • A correction was requested.
  • An endorsement was issued.
  • The wrong variant allegedly remained.
  • The customer again raised the issue before any accident occurred.

So if an accident happens while the correction is still pending, the customer wants to know:

Why should the entire consequence fall on the customer when the insurer had already been informed about the discrepancy before the accident?

What about the used-car dealer?

The dealer's role may also need to be examined. The insurance was purchased through the dealer, and therefore the relevant question is also: What information did the dealer provide to the insurer?

If incorrect information was entered or supplied by the dealer/intermediary, that may become relevant. The complete chain of documents should therefore be examined rather than simply looking at the final policy.

The most important evidence

  • RC
  • Used-car purchase invoice
  • Original insurance policy
  • Proposal/application documents, if available
  • Email requesting correction
  • Insurer's response
  • Endorsement/revised policy
  • Second correction request
  • Grievance complaint and acknowledgement
  • Dealer communications
  • Any document establishing the actual vehicle variant
