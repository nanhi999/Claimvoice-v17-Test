Theft Claim Rejected as “No-Response” — Insurance Ombudsman Orders Cash Settlement

A consumer's real experience with a motor insurance theft claim

Verified consumer story: This story was shared with ClaimVoice by the client/consumer. ClaimVoice reviewed the Insurance Ombudsman award/document relating to the matter before publishing this story. Sensitive information has been masked to protect the client's privacy.

What happened?

The insured, Sxxxxxm, xxxx, Jind, had a motor insurance policy covering a Hxxxxx xxxx xE car.

The vehicle was stolen from Uxxxx xxxxr, Delhi, on 16 June 2024. The award records that the theft was intimated to the insurer on 19 June 2024. A police FIR was registered on 26 June 2024. The final “Untraced” report was later accepted judicially by the ACJM Court, Dwarka, Delhi, on 3 August 2024.

The claim was investigated

The insurer's own appointed investigator and internal claims department verified the relevant facts, police records, original keys and court documents.

According to the Insurance Ombudsman award, the investigation concluded that the theft claim was genuine.

A replacement vehicle was offered later

The insurer made offers for a similar used/replacement vehicle on 15 October 2024 and 23 October 2024.

By that time, the consumer stated that he had already arranged another vehicle because a working vehicle was needed for his family. He therefore did not accept the replacement vehicle and sought settlement of the theft claim on a cash/IDV basis.

The claim was closed as “Rejected due to No-Response”

The insurer relied on Policy Condition No. 3 and maintained that it had an option to repair, reinstate, replace the vehicle or make a cash payment. The insurer also raised issues concerning the timing of intimation/FIR and the safeguarding of the vehicle.

After the replacement option was not accepted, the insurer formally closed the claim on 13 November 2024 as:

“Rejected due to No-Response”

The consumer approached the Insurance Ombudsman

The consumer challenged the repudiation before the Insurance Ombudsman, Chandigarh.

The award records that the matter was considered on the documents and submissions placed before the forum.

What did the Insurance Ombudsman find?

The Ombudsman recorded that the theft of the insured vehicle was undisputed. The insurer's own investigator and internal claims department had verified the relevant facts and concluded that the claim was genuine.

The Ombudsman also considered the replacement offers made several months after the theft. The award found that the insurer's discretion under Policy Condition No. 3 could not be exercised arbitrarily to the prejudice of the insured.

The award specifically concluded that the policy did not empower the insurer to force the insured to accept a second-hand/replacement vehicle against his wishes in the circumstances of this case.

Cash settlement ordered

The Insurance Ombudsman held that the insurer's failure to disburse the IDV, together with the belated attempt to force a second-hand vehicle replacement, constituted a service deficiency.

The consumer was therefore held entitled to settlement of the theft claim on a cash disbursement of IDV basis.

The award was dated 12 August 2026 and directed compliance within 30 days. It further stated that delay in honouring the award beyond 30 days would attract a penalty of ₹5,000 per day, as specified in the award.

What can consumers learn from this case?

  • Keep the policy schedule and all claim communications safely.
  • Keep records of theft intimation, FIR, police reports and court documents.
  • If an insurer offers a replacement vehicle instead of cash settlement, read the policy wording and ask for the basis of the offer in writing.
  • If a claim is closed or rejected, ask for the decision and reasons in writing.
  • Do not ignore insurer communications. Keep a written record of every response.
  • If the matter is not resolved through the insurer's grievance process, consider the appropriate external grievance-redressal mechanism.

ClaimVoice takeaway

This case is a useful example of why consumers should not treat a claim closure as the end of the matter. The final outcome depends on the policy, facts, documents and the findings of the forum dealing with the dispute. In this particular award, the Ombudsman found a service deficiency and directed cash settlement on an IDV basis.
