Theft Claim Rejected as “No-Response” — Insurance Ombudsman Orders Cash Settlement

A consumer's real experience with a motor insurance theft claim

Verified consumer story: This story was shared with ClaimVoice by the client/consumer. ClaimVoice reviewed and verified the Insurance Ombudsman award/document relating to the matter before publishing this story. Sensitive information has been masked to protect the client's privacy.

What happened?

The insured, Shubham, xxxx, Jind, had a motor insurance policy covering a Hyundai xxxx Fe car.

The vehicle was stolen from Delhi on 16 June 2024.

The theft was reported to the insurer, and a police FIR was subsequently registered. The police later submitted an “Untraced” report, which was judicially accepted by the concerned court. The Insurance Ombudsman award records these facts as part of the case history.

The claim was investigated

An important part of this case was that the insurer's own appointed investigator examined the matter.

According to the Insurance Ombudsman award, the investigator verified the relevant facts, police records, FIR, original keys and court documents.

The investigator concluded that the theft claim was genuine and negligence-free.

Then the dispute started

According to the consumer's submission, the required claim documents had been provided and the claim continued to remain under process.

The insurer subsequently made an offer to provide a similar used/replacement vehicle.

However, the replacement offer came several months after the theft.

By that time, the consumer stated that he had already arranged another vehicle because a functional vehicle was necessary for his family.

He therefore did not accept the replacement vehicle and instead requested settlement of the theft claim on a cash/IDV basis.

Why did the insurer close the claim?

The insurer took a different position.

It pointed to delays in informing the insurer and registering the police FIR. It also argued that the insured had failed to exercise adequate care in safeguarding the vehicle.

The insurer relied upon Policy Condition No. 3, stating that it had the option to repair, reinstate, replace the vehicle or make a cash payment.

The insurer had made replacement offers in October 2024. When the replacement option was not accepted, the claim was eventually closed as:

“Rejected due to No-Response”

The insurer maintained that its claim-handling process was in accordance with the policy and applicable procedures.

The consumer approached the Insurance Ombudsman

The consumer did not accept the closure of the claim and approached the Insurance Ombudsman, Chandigarh.

The matter was heard by the Ombudsman and both sides were given an opportunity to present their respective positions.

What did the Insurance Ombudsman find?

The Ombudsman examined the facts and documents placed before it.

One of the most important findings was that the theft itself was undisputed.

The award also recorded that the insurer's own investigator had verified the relevant facts and concluded that the claim was genuine and negligence-free.

The Ombudsman further noted that the insurer had made the replacement offer around four months after the loss. By that time, the complainant had already arranged an alternative vehicle.

The award therefore considered the delayed replacement offer irrelevant in the circumstances of the case.

Could the insured be forced to accept a replacement vehicle?

This became a key issue.

The Ombudsman considered Policy Condition No. 3, under which the insurer had an option to repair, reinstate, replace or make a cash payment.

However, the award observed that this discretion could not be exercised arbitrarily to the prejudice of the insured.

The Ombudsman specifically concluded that the policy did not empower the insurer to force the insured to accept a replacement vehicle against his wishes in the circumstances of this case.

The Ombudsman consequently found a service deficiency and held that the consumer was entitled to settlement of the claim on a cash/IDV basis.

What was awarded?

The Insurance Ombudsman directed the insurer to settle the theft claim by making a cash payment based on the vehicle's IDV, after deducting the applicable compulsory deductible.

What can other vehicle owners learn from this case?

1. Keep every document

FIR, police reports, court documents, policy documents, claim correspondence, investigation reports and communications with the insurer can become extremely important if a claim is disputed.

2. Always ask why a claim has been rejected

A policyholder should not stop at the word “Rejected.”

Ask: Why was it rejected? Which policy condition is being relied upon? What evidence supports the rejection?

3. A replacement offer and cash settlement are not necessarily the same thing

This case shows why the circumstances and timing of a replacement offer can become important. The Ombudsman specifically considered that the replacement offer came months after the theft, when the consumer had already arranged another vehicle.

4. Keep communication in writing

Whenever possible, maintain emails, letters, claim-status communications and responses from the insurer. A written record can help establish what was requested, what was submitted and what response was received.

5. An investigation does not automatically mean the claim is invalid

In this case, the award recorded that the insurer's own investigator had verified the relevant records and found the claim genuine and negligence-free.

6. Escalation is available when a claim dispute remains unresolved

If an insured believes that a claim has been wrongly rejected or closed, the person can use the insurer's grievance process and, where applicable, approach the appropriate external redressal mechanism.

ClaimVoice Takeaway

The important lesson from this case is not that an insurer can never offer a replacement vehicle.

The lesson is that policy conditions and settlement options have to be considered in the actual circumstances of the claim.

If your motor insurance claim is rejected, closed or offered an unexpected settlement, don't simply accept or reject it without understanding the reason.

  • Ask for the decision in writing.
  • Read the relevant policy condition.
  • Collect your documents.
  • Check what the investigation actually concluded.
  • If necessary, use the available grievance and dispute-resolution process.
