Motor Insurance Claim Documents Checklist for Accident and Theft Claims

What documents are required for a motor insurance claim? There is no single universal list that applies to every accident, theft, fire or own-damage claim. The insurer may ask for documents based on the policy, type of loss, vehicle ownership, driver, police action, investigation and settlement process. This page gives an approximate practical checklist so a policyholder can prepare a claim file without guessing.

Important: This is an approximate awareness checklist, not a guarantee that a particular insurer will request every document listed. Requirements can change with the facts of the claim. Ask the insurer or concerned authority for the specific documents applicable to your case.

Accident / Own-Damage Claim Documents

For a typical motor accident or own-damage claim, keep the following records ready where applicable:

Document / recordWhy it may matter
Policy schedule / certificateShows the policy period, vehicle and selected cover. The full policy wording and endorsements also matter.
RC / registration recordHelps establish the insured vehicle's registration and identity.
Driving licenceMay be relevant to driver eligibility and claim assessment.
Claim form / claim intimationCreates the formal claim record and captures basic loss details.
Claim number / acknowledgementUseful for tracking submissions and later correspondence.
Accident photographs and videoCan help establish damage, location and condition before repair.
Repair estimateShows proposed repairs and estimated cost; it is not automatically the final payable amount.
Survey / assessment documentsUseful for understanding what was inspected, assessed or questioned.
Repair invoice and payment proofShows work actually carried out and amounts charged/paid.
FIR / police record, where applicableMay be relevant in serious accidents, injuries, disputes or other circumstances requiring police reporting.
Medical / MLC documents, where applicableMay be relevant when injury or medical treatment forms part of the circumstances.
Bank details / cancelled cheque, where requiredMay be requested for settlement payment or verification.
Additional documents requested by insurerSpecific cases may require further evidence or clarification.

Vehicle Theft Claim Documents

A theft claim usually requires a stronger documentary trail because the insurer and authorities need to establish the reported theft, vehicle identity, ownership and investigation status.

Document / recordWhy it may matter
Policy schedule / certificateConfirms the insurance cover and policy period.
RC / registration recordEstablishes vehicle identity and registered details.
FIR / police complaintRecords the reported theft and circumstances.
Police investigation / final report documents, where applicableMay be required depending on the stage and process of the theft investigation.
All available vehicle keysMay be requested to establish key availability and circumstances of theft.
Claim form / claim intimationCreates the insurer's theft claim record.
Claim acknowledgement / referenceHelps track communication and document submission.
Driving licence, where relevantMay be requested as part of claim verification.
Bank details / cancelled cheque, where requiredMay be required for settlement.
RTO forms / ownership-transfer documents, where requiredMay become relevant during settlement, transfer or recovery procedures.
Financier / hypothecation documents, where applicableMay be relevant when the vehicle has outstanding finance.
Other documents requested during investigationThe exact requirements depend on the facts and policy terms.

Documents are not automatically proof of wrongdoing

An insurer asking for an additional document does not by itself mean that a claim has been rejected or that the claim is fraudulent. A document request may be part of normal assessment or investigation. The useful approach is to ask what issue the document is intended to clarify and keep a written record of what was submitted.

How to submit claim documents safely

  1. Keep a copy of every document before submission.
  2. Use the insurer's official email, portal, branch or other recognised submission channel.
  3. Record the date and method of submission.
  4. Keep the acknowledgement or claim reference.
  5. Do not send passwords, OTPs or unnecessary sensitive information.
  6. If a document is unavailable, explain why in writing and ask what alternative evidence is acceptable.
