Why Motor Insurance Claims Get Rejected or Paid Partly in India

A practical ClaimVoice guide • Simple English • Technical terms explained with examples

Buying motor insurance does not mean every loss will automatically be paid. A claim can be rejected, partly settled, delayed or disputed. The reason should be checked against the policy wording, the facts and the evidence.

The ClaimVoice rule:

Do not stop at “claim rejected”. Ask: What is the exact reason? What policy term is being used? Does that term actually apply to what happened?

1. Policy was not active on the date of loss

Policy period
In simple words: the dates for which the insurance cover is active.
Example: The policy expired on 10 August and the accident happened on 15 August. The own-damage claim can face a basic coverage problem because the policy was not active on the accident date.

2. Driving licence problem

Driving licence condition
In simple words: the driver must meet the licence requirements applicable to the vehicle and the law.

An insurer may check the licence when assessing a claim. The exact effect of a licence issue depends on the facts, policy wording and applicable law. Do not assume that every licence objection automatically decides the entire claim.

3. Vehicle modification was not disclosed

Material modification
In simple words: a change to the vehicle that may affect its risk, identity, use or the information given to the insurer.

Examples include aftermarket electrical wiring, unusual tyre sizes, alloy-wheel changes, sunroof conversion, performance changes or an LPG/CNG installation. Whether a modification affects a claim depends on the modification, the policy, endorsements and the cause of the loss.

Example: A car has aftermarket electrical wiring and later suffers an electrical fire. The insurer may investigate whether the modification is connected to the fire. That is different from saying that every modified car automatically has no insurance cover.

4. Depreciation reduced the settlement

Depreciation
In simple words: an age/use-related reduction applied to specified parts or values when the claim is calculated, according to the policy.

A standard policy may apply depreciation to eligible parts. A Zero Depreciation or Nil Depreciation add-on can change this treatment for covered parts, but it does not automatically remove every deductible, exclusion or non-covered item.

5. The add-on you expected was not purchased

Add-on
In simple words: extra cover bought in addition to the base policy.

Examples include Zero Depreciation, Engine Protection, Consumables, Return to Invoice and Roadside Assistance. Each add-on has its own wording, limits and exclusions.

6. Flood or water entered the engine

Hydrostatic lock
In simple words: water enters the engine cylinder and the engine cannot rotate normally.

Flood-related engine damage can raise questions about the cause of damage, the way the vehicle was driven and whether a suitable add-on applies. If a vehicle stops after entering deep water, repeatedly trying to restart it can increase damage.

7. Major repairs started before inspection

Survey / loss assessment
In simple words: the insurer's appointed surveyor assesses the damage and the likely claim amount.

Where practical and safe, preserve the condition of the damaged vehicle until the insurer has had a reasonable opportunity to inspect it. Take photographs and keep damaged parts where they may be needed as evidence.

8. Claim was not intimated promptly

Claim intimation
In simple words: telling the insurer about the loss or accident.

Inform the insurer as soon as reasonably possible. A delay should not be described as an automatic rejection in every case; the reason for the delay and its effect on the insurer's investigation can matter.

9. Private car was used for commercial purposes

Vehicle-use condition
In simple words: the vehicle should be used in the manner permitted by the policy and law.
Example: A car insured for private use is being used as a paid taxi when an accident occurs. The insurer may examine the use of the vehicle as part of the claim decision.

10. CNG or LPG details were not updated

After a CNG/LPG installation, the owner should check the vehicle records and insurance policy requirements. A mismatch between the actual vehicle and the policy/records can create a claim dispute.

11. Missing or inconsistent evidence

Insurance claims are assessed using documents and evidence. Depending on the loss, this can include the policy, registration certificate, driving licence, FIR, photographs, repair estimates, invoices and other documents relevant to the incident.

Important: An insurer asking for additional documents does not by itself mean the claim has been rejected. Ask why each additional document is needed and keep a record of what you submit.

Rejection, partial settlement and delay are different

SituationSimple meaning
RejectedInsurer says the claim is not payable.
Partly settledInsurer accepts some amount but applies deductions or excludes some items.
DelayedClaim decision or payment has not been completed within the expected process.
DisputedCustomer and insurer disagree about coverage, amount or facts.

What to do if your claim is rejected

  1. Ask for the reason in writing.
  2. Ask for the exact policy clause, exclusion or condition being relied upon.
  3. Compare that wording with what actually happened.
  4. Check whether the insurer has considered the evidence you submitted.
  5. If you disagree, use the insurer's grievance process and then the appropriate escalation route.
