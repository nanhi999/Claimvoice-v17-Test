— POLICY CHECK —

Policy Issued Does Not Mean Everything Is Correct

Receiving your motor insurance policy is not the end of the checking process. The policy document contains important information about you and your vehicle. A small error may look harmless today but can create unnecessary questions when you later need to make a claim.

Golden rule: As soon as you receive your policy, compare its important particulars with your RC and the information you provided while purchasing the policy. If you find any discrepancy, report it to the concerned insurer immediately and keep a written record of the correction request.

1. Do not assume: “The policy has been issued, so everything must be correct.”

A policy can be issued even when a particular entered in the document needs correction. This does not automatically mean that a future claim will be rejected, but an incorrect or inconsistent record can create avoidable questions during claim processing, verification or investigation.

The safest approach is simple: check the policy when you receive it, not when you need it.

2. What should you check immediately?

Do not only check the premium and policy dates. Compare the policy with your RC and other vehicle records, especially the following:

☐ Insured / policyholder name
☐ Complete address
☐ Vehicle registration number
☐ Chassis number
☐ Engine number
☐ Vehicle make
☐ Model
☐ Variant
☐ Year of manufacture
☐ Date of registration
☐ Engine capacity (CC)
☐ Fuel type
☐ Policy start & end dates
☐ IDV
☐ Add-ons selected
☐ Other important vehicle particulars

For a motor policy, even details that appear to be “technical” can be important. Do not ignore a mismatch simply because the vehicle itself is correctly identified by its registration number.

3. The most important comparison: Policy vs RC

Keep the policy document and the vehicle's RC together and compare the vehicle particulars line by line. If the policy shows a different chassis number, engine number, make, model, variant, manufacturing year, registration date or engine capacity, raise the issue promptly.

Also check the insured person's name and address. If you have moved, changed details or noticed a spelling mistake, ask the insurer to update the record through its prescribed process.

⚠️ Do not wait for a claim to discover the mistake

The best time to identify a policy discrepancy is immediately after policy issuance. Finding an error at the time of a theft or accident can make an already stressful claim more complicated.

4. What if you find a discrepancy?

Contact the concerned insurance company through its official customer-service, policy-service or other authorised channel. Clearly state:

  1. Your policy number.
  2. The incorrect detail appearing on the policy.
  3. The correct detail as shown in the RC or other supporting document.
  4. That you are requesting rectification of the policy record/document.
  5. The supporting document, where appropriate.

Where possible, make the request in writing or use a channel that provides a trackable service request.

5. Always obtain a Ticket / Service Request Number

This is one of the most important practical steps.

When you report the discrepancy, ask the insurer for a proper Ticket Number, Service Request Number, Complaint Number or other unique reference number. Do not rely only on “we have informed the concerned team” or a verbal assurance.

Write the number on your policy copy.
For example: “Correction requested — SR No. ______ — Date: ______”

You should also save the email, SMS, screenshot or acknowledgement through which the request was registered.

6. Keep a correction trail

After raising the request, maintain a small record. It can be as simple as a note on your phone or a folder containing the documents.

☐ Policy copy with discrepancy marked
☐ Ticket / SR number recorded
☐ Date of correction request
☐ Email / SMS / acknowledgement saved
☐ Supporting RC/document saved
☐ Follow-up dates recorded
☐ Corrected policy / endorsement saved
☐ Final confirmation retained

If the insurer asks for an additional document, keep proof of submission. If the request remains pending, follow up using the same reference number.

7. Follow up until the correction is actually completed

Opening a service request is only the first step. Do not assume the record has been corrected merely because a ticket was created.

Follow up with the insurer and check the updated policy, endorsement or other confirmation provided by the insurer. Compare the corrected document again to make sure the mistake has actually been rectified.

If the insurer closes the request without correcting the detail, ask for the reason in writing and raise the matter again through the appropriate grievance channel if necessary.

8. Why is this so important for a future claim?

Motor insurance claims can involve documents and records from different sources: the policy, RC, police documents, repair records, vehicle inspection, invoices and other evidence. Inconsistencies between records can lead to additional questions.

That is why a policyholder should not simply keep the policy PDF in an email inbox and forget about it. Check it, correct it, document the correction and keep the final version.

This is especially useful where the discrepancy concerns the vehicle's identity or specification, such as the chassis number, engine number, make, model, variant or registration-related particulars.

9. What if the mistake was already present when the policy was issued?

Report it as soon as you notice it. Explain what is incorrect and provide the correct information and supporting record where available.

Do not try to alter, edit or manually change the insurance policy yourself. A policy document should be corrected through the insurer's authorised process, with the resulting endorsement, revised policy or written confirmation preserved for your records.

10. A simple policy-check routine

Make this a habit every time you buy, renew or receive a changed motor insurance policy:

  1. Receive the policy.
  2. Compare it with the RC.
  3. Check the name and address.
  4. Check all major vehicle particulars.
  5. Check policy dates, IDV and add-ons.
  6. Report every discrepancy promptly.
  7. Obtain a Ticket / Service Request Number.
  8. Write the number and date on your policy copy.
  9. Follow up until rectification is confirmed.
  10. Save the corrected policy/endorsement and the complete communication trail.

Quick takeaway

📌 Check today — not at claim time

Policy issued ≠ policy checked.

Take a few minutes after receiving your policy to verify the important details. If something is wrong, report it immediately, obtain a trackable reference number, write that number on your policy copy, follow up, and keep evidence of the correction.
